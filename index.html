Our Story

Harry and Caroline Kroll. The basement under the Labor Temple. Three generations of the Wier family.

The story starts in 1931, when Harry and Caroline Kroll began serving meals at a family-run hotel in Green Bay. The food was popular enough that in 1936 they opened the first official Kroll's restaurant at 1658 Main Street. That is where the Kroll's Hamburger went on the menu, on the charcoal grill, finished with butter, on a toasted bun. The version every Wisconsin butter burger ever served has been measured against since.

Before World War II the restaurant moved to the basement under the Labor Temple Auditorium. Harry's daughter Pat kept the basement line running for years, then moved the operation south. In 1974, the address became 1990 S. Ridge Road, directly across from Lambeau Field, where it has been since.

The third generation runs the kitchen today. Bobbie Van Der Perren Wier and her husband Mike Wier are the current stewards. Mike started working at Kroll's at 15. He has been in the building for nearly six decades. The grill is still charcoal. The chili is still made in the morning. The fish fry is still hand-battered on Friday.

A note on the name.

There are two Kroll's restaurants in Green Bay. They share a family origin and the 1936 founding by Harry and Caroline Kroll, and they are separately owned and operated. Kroll's West, the restaurant on S. Ridge Road across from Lambeau Field, has been stewarded by the Wier family for three generations. If you are looking for the original Wier-family Kroll's West, you are in the right place.

The Butter Burger

The Wisconsin butter burger, cooked the way it was meant to be.

Wisconsin has more than one butter burger. Solly's in Milwaukee built theirs on the griddle. Kroll's built theirs on the charcoal grill. Both are right. Only one of them was on a Green Bay menu in 1936.

01

Live charcoal, not a flat top

The grill is fired with Kingsford. The smoke gets into the patty. That is what makes a Kroll's burger taste different from a butter burger you eat anywhere else.

02

Finished with real butter, not margarine

A pat of butter melts into the patty as it comes off the grill. The bun gets buttered too. The fat is the point.

03

Toasted bun, never steamed

The bun is buttered and pressed on the grill until the crust is set. It holds the patty, the butter, and the toppings without going soft.

04

The recipe has not changed since 1936

Three generations of the Wier family have kept the same method. The Kroll's Hamburger Harry and Caroline put on the menu in 1936 is the one you are ordering today.

Packers gameday

Pregame, postgame, every Sunday in Green Bay.

Lambeau Field opens for Packers home games nine times a year. Every Sunday morning at Kroll's looks the same: green-and-gold filling the dining room, the bar three deep, charcoal burgers off the grill, broasted chicken trays going out the door.

The Lambeau dining guides on packers.com and onmilwaukee.com both name Kroll's West as the pregame stop. The road from the parking lot to the stadium runs past our front door. The fans who walk in are not strangers.

Friday in Wisconsin

The Wisconsin Friday fish fry, done the way it was meant to be done.

Wisconsin has a Friday tradition. Every supper club, tavern, parish hall, and family kitchen runs a fish fry at the end of the week. Kroll's West has been part of that line since the original menu.

Hand-battered cod, potato pancakes or french fries, rye bread, coleslaw, tartar. The order has not changed. The rye bread is on the table before the basket. The tartar is made in the kitchen.

Walk-in seating most of the night. Reservation calls welcome for tables of six or more. Friday is the busiest night of the week, and that is on purpose.
